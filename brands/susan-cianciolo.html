Founder story

She illustrated for Geoffrey Beene, did Club USA flyers, lasted briefly at Badgley Mischka, then took a part-time production job at Kim Gordon’s X-Girl while launching RUN. The name: she was a teenage runner and wanted to run from everything that exists. Shows were sewing circles and gallery installations — first one at Andrea Rosen in 1995, not a runway. Chloë Sevigny walked.

For RUN 11 she rented a Chelsea storefront, street-cast musicians and ex-models, printed fake Run money with Chris Johanson, and ran a one-day guerrilla shop in New York and one at the Purple Institute in Paris. In 2001 the New York Times wrote Designer Packs It In; she chose art over the fashion calendar. Eckhaus Latta later put her on their runway as living lineage.

“I was in my early 20s and I felt so fearless and invincible. I wanted to run from everything that exists, [to be] subversive, to be against the system.”

Vogue oral history of RUN · 2017

“Run equals ‘floating,’ so that you are not a part of whatever else exists and you’re running so far ahead—really running away from anything and everything. You are doing your own work that comes from you.”
