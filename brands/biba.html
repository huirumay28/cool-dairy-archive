Founder story

Fitz told her illustration was dying and pushed her into design. The name is her sister Biruta’s nickname. Breakthrough: a pink gingham sleeveless shift and matching headscarf, inspired by a Bardot press photo, featured by Felicity Green in the Daily Mirror in 1964. They expected about 3,000 orders and got 17,000 (V&A figure; Hulanicki has also said 70,000 and, in W, 90,000 — disputed. Use 17,000 for the fact card).

The first shop was a former chemist, interiors like a fin-de-siècle dressing-up box — that last phrase is a journalist’s, not hers. Big Biba filled seven Art Deco floors. In 1971 the Angry Brigade bombed the Kensington High Street shop — they thought fashion enslaved women; Hulanicki thought she was building a haven for rebels on a budget.

“You don’t realize how bad things were in England in that period. You had this incredible generation that had money to spend and nowhere to spend it. We wanted something to wear out dancing, but there was nothing but frumpy old-lady clothes. And fashion was just for ladies who lunched.”

W Magazine, Originals issue · 2021

“Biba attracted troublemakers — people who liked to make trouble.”

BBC Culture · 2024
