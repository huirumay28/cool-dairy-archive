Label (Laura Whitcomb)

Laura Whitcomb (born 1970, Los Angeles), self-taught, a music-video stylist before she was a designer.

Founded 1989 (per the V&A); first shop in Soho / Lafayette Street, New York, 1994; later Label NYC. Added 23 Nov 2026 digest. · Woman-founded and woman-led: Laura Whitcomb. Backed for a time by the rap label Tommy Boy (Vogue, 1994) — say so. · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

Founder story

Whitcomb grew up in east Los Angeles, went to school in London from about 14 to 19 and lived with Central Saint Martins and London College of Fashion students, which is where she says she half-learned to make clothes; the V&A adds that she was inspired by the DIY nature of punk (V&A; Complex, 2023). Back in LA she styled videos for the Red Hot Chili Peppers, Cypress Hill and The Pharcyde’s “Passing Me By”, where she says she first showed the adidas dress.

Her first collection turned the cotton vests worn by women in east LA, printed with nicknames in Old English type, into dresses: “I wanted to just create an extension of masculine ethos moving into something very female… we saw the dance floor as a forum to promote new ideologies” (Complex). The name and packaging copied generic supermarket own-brand labels from Ralphs — the baby-blue and navy stripe on a generic beer can — the same visual joke as Repo Man.

The adidas dress came from a Paris warehouse party where young men wore adidas tracksuits with cravats tucked into the zip and berets; she wanted the woman’s version, sports brand crossed with evening shape. She made the first ones herself, with custom two-way zips. Guy Oseary showed one to Madonna, who wore them to Knicks games; adidas called back excited and a licence followed — among the first designer collaborations with a sportswear giant (V&A; Complex). The LA earthquake destroyed her production and knock-offs moved in. Versions were worn by Madonna, Kim Gordon and Queen Latifah (V&A). Vogue, January 1994, credited Kim Gordon with taking adidas out at night and named Label’s “zip-up-the-front warm-up-suit dress in shiny baby blue nylon” as the club dress code.

In 2022 Alessandro Michele reworked the dress for Gucci’s adidas collection “Exquisite”, after contacting Whitcomb and inviting her to the show; Madonna’s son David Banda later wore the Gucci version (V&A; Complex). Whitcomb has since left fashion and curates art exhibitions. In 2025 she gave a dress to the V&A (T.125-2025).

Style codes

  • Floor-length, long-sleeved, body-skimming sports jersey dress
  • Three white stripes down the sleeves, trefoil at the chest, white V-neck trim
  • Sports codes recut as evening: tracksuit to gown, boxing shorts to gown, uniforms (Metro, UPS) to dresses
  • Generic-packaging branding: lower-case ‘label’ tag with baby-blue and navy stripe
  • Worn with adidas Gazelles or Puma Suedes, downtown 1993–94

Signature pieces

  • adidas dress, black with white stripes and trefoil, 1993 (V&A T.125-2025) — Full-length, long-sleeved; given by Laura Whitcomb. The V&A calls it an early, if not the first, collaboration between a major sportswear brand and a fashion label.
  • Red adidas-stripe maxi dress worn by Madonna — Per Complex, 2023; she wore different Label dresses four times, per Whitcomb.
  • Baby-blue shiny nylon zip-front warm-up-suit dress — Named in Vogue, January 1994.
  • Wife-beater dresses (first collection) — Homage to the women of east Los Angeles; nicknames in Old English type.
