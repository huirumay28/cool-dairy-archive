Founder story

Kerrigan moved to New York in 1986 and worked in film costume and wardrobe, on Jim Jarmusch’s Mystery Train and on My Cousin Vinny. She was also a picker for Reminiscence, the thrift store on lower Fifth Avenue, pulling clothes out of fabric-sorted bins in the Bronx (NextTribe). “Clothing in New York at that time was a pretty dismal affair,” she told the Observer in 1997, so she made her own or reworked vintage.

In 1991, with about $40,000 in savings and no fashion-industry experience, she opened a shop on East Sixth Street with a production studio in the back. A pair of flocked-velvet men’s vintage trousers from one of those Bronx runs (Wikipedia says red velvet 70s hip-huggers) became her first design: a low-slung boot-cut she made “six to eight pairs” of a day. Kim Gordon was an early fan and became a friend; stylists and editors such as Camilla Nickerson followed. Vogue featured her in 1995 and she won the CFDA Perry Ellis Award in 1996.

In 1997 she opened 21 Bond Street in William Wegman’s former studio, launched a second line, K-189, and showed at the Pitt Street pool. In 1999 she sold a majority stake to Pegasus Apparel Group; renamed Leiber Group, it stopped producing Daryl K in 2001. She won the labels back and relaunched in October 2002. She closed Bond Street on 30 March 2012 (“I’m beginning another chapter”) and now makes a small Evergreen collection, best known for stretch lamb-leather leggings.

In November–December 2025 the curator Nina Stritzler-Levine staged “Daryl K I Am My Muse” at VERSE Work/Shop in Red Hook, NY: about 50 pieces from Kerrigan’s own archive in four themes — New York, Rebel, Woman, I Am My Muse — on forms Paul Leonard made by hand. Her line to Vogue: “Female designers are not in there designing for the females.”

Style codes

  • Low-slung hip-hugger boot-cut jeans and trousers (“low riders”)
  • Second-skin stretch leather leggings and pants
  • Tailoring crossed with activewear — clothes for the subway, a bicycle and dancing all night (VERSE)
  • Thrift-picker fabric sense: reworked vintage, repurposed materials
  • Downtown 90s minimalism: grey hip-huggers, layered knits, suede cowboy boots with carved wood heels (Kerrigan’s own outfit, Observer 1997)

Signature pieces

  • Hip Hugger Bootleg Jean, 1991– — The cult piece; the brand names Kim Gordon as an early wearer.
  • Stretch leather leggings — Now the Evergreen collection staple.
  • K-189 line, 1997 — The second, lower-priced line (Observer).
