Founder story

Added 2 Nov 2026 digest. Coppola was 22. She had spent teenage summers around the Chanel atelier and was friends with Anna Sui and Marc Jacobs. The push came from X-Girl: “When I helped Kim Gordon and Daisy von Furth put on the X-Girl show in SoHo, I discovered they didn’t know how to sew, either. I realized I admire people who just jump into things and do it.” She designed; Linda Meltzer, whose Tease Tee’s had made the first shrunken ‘baby Ts’, was “the grown-up.”

The first line was about 20 pieces: baby tees printed ‘Wasted’ and ‘I Love Booze’, nylon T-dresses stencilled with Che Guevara or Steve McQueen, hip-hugger pinstripe trousers and skirts, nylon necktie dresses, and ‘The Lady Skirt’, a silk charmeuse slip with a little lace at the hem. Inspiration: men she saw in Central America wearing faded T-shirts with suit trousers. She refused the ‘little girl line’ label — “I’m more into the ‘Mom sensibility’” — and wanted it to fit every body even though the samples were cut on her. Anna Sui gave her a list of stores. Stockists in 1994: Bloomingdale’s (with the Lexington Avenue windows), X-Girl, Stussy Union, Fred Segal Melrose and Supreme on Lafayette.

Per the Japanese owner: a Shibuya PARCO pop-up in 1997, then the first shop in Daikanyama, Tokyo, in 1999 — before Los Angeles — named Heaven 27 after Coppola and Hayman’s band. It was dressed like a girl’s bedroom: nail polish, candy, souvenir keyrings, photo books and CDs next to the clothes, which the company links to her later films. Jeff McFetridge was art director. In 2002 Coppola stepped away to concentrate on film and the Japanese company took the brand over.

Style codes

  • Shrunken baby tees with deadpan slogans
  • Nylon T-dresses with stencilled portraits
  • Low-rise pinstripe trousers and skirts — menswear suiting worn with faded tees
  • Bias slip skirts with a small lace hem
  • 1990s LA/NY/Tokyo girl-brand graphics; Jeff McFetridge art direction

Signature pieces

  • ‘Wasted’ / ‘I Love Booze’ baby tees, 1994 — Debut line, per W.
