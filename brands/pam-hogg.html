Founder story

Hogg made her own clothes from age six because hand-me-downs were the budget. In late-70s London the Blitz door policy was the filter: she designed outfits wild enough to get past Steve Strange, then people asked to buy them. Psychedelic Jungle (1981) was New Wave acid, sold from a Hyper Hyper stall.

She dressed Debbie Harry, Siouxsie, Paula Yates, Ian Astbury, even Princess Diana, and refused to “sell out” to the mainstream. She also fronted bands. Catsuits earned her “Queen of Cling” — other people’s nickname (Bella Freud: “superstars from the planet Venus”). Do not put it in Hogg’s mouth.

“I was customising all the hand-me-downs from a really early age but I never really focused on it. We didn’t have enough money for me to choose so it was more a survival thing, cutting things up and rearranging creating my own identity.”

AnOther

“That is the big one because it was there right from the start, and still is. When I started at art school I did fine art and was actually drawing on my body. I thought this is like a catsuit, so I made simple catsuits, then developed it.”
