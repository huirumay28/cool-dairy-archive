X-Girl

Kim Gordon (Sonic Youth) + Daisy von Furth. Mike D / X-Large circle asked them to do a girls’ line.

Founded 1993 as a project; launched summer 1994. Original run to about 1998; later licensed in Japan. · Women-founded: yes. · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

Founder story

Gordon and von Furth were already shopping deadstock together — Army-Navy in Danbury, football tees on 14th Street, the perfect 70s Levi’s 517 cord. X-Large’s Eric Bonerz asked them to design a fitted girls’ answer to baggy skate menswear. Mike Mills drew the face logo from his friend Michelle Rockwood.

Two days after Kurt Cobain died, Sofia Coppola and Spike Jonze staged a guerrilla show on the street in SoHo, timed to steal traffic from Marc Jacobs; Chloë Sevigny walked; J Mascis sat in the audience. It was the anti-runway: a painted sheet, no VIP list. Gordon and von Furth left after the 90s run; Japanese corporate ownership continues. Vintage 1994–98 US pieces are the ones that matter.

“Doing a girl’s line for X-Large was Eric’s idea, and he asked Daisy and I if we’d do it. He knew we were interested in fashion. We were always going to thrift stores and looking for like the perfect ’70s 517 Levi’s cords or talking about the way T-shirts fit.”

Kim Gordon, Paper oral history · 2012

“I don’t think we thought we were doing anything important or different. I think we just thought we were making really bad clothes… It’s always a challenge to make things that you can wear every day that are cool. That was the number one driving force or idea with X-Girl.”

Kim Gordon, Paper · 2012

“It was always trying to find what was the least cool thing, and making it cool.”

Daisy von Furth, Paper · 2012
