Zandra Rhodes

Zandra Rhodes (born 1940, Chatham, Kent), textile designer trained at Medway College of Art and the Royal College of Art.

Founder story

Her mother was a fitter at the House of Worth in Paris and later taught at Medway College of Art, where a tutor steered Zandra into textile design; a scholarship took her to the Royal College of Art (Wikipedia; The Guardian). “I was proud to be a textile designer, and I did not feel I was inferior to a painter or a sculptor,” she later wrote. From 1969 she made floating, screen-printed chiffon under her own name, with green and later pink hair to match. Freddie Mercury and Brian May came to her attic studio off Portobello Road at night in 1974 because she had no changing room.

In 1977 she turned to punk for one collection, Conceptual Chic: black, shocking-pink and bright-red rayon jersey cut with deliberate tears, the raw edges zigzag-stitched, decorated with beaded safety pins, the steel ball-chain used for sink plugs, and diamanté (V&A; UCA Zandra Rhodes Digital Study Collection). She called it “the year I became the infamous High Priestess of Punk”. The New York Times wrote in September 1977 that she “catapulted ‘punk rock’ to the fashion world’s attention”, though with little of its aggression. The Washington Post noted the dresses cost about $600 and sold “rather well”.

She is honest that no real punk would have given her the time of day (The Guardian). Besides the street, she named Schiaparelli and Dalí’s 1938 Tears dress and her own 1971 Elizabethan slashed-silk collection as influences (UCA). One story told about the collection, which the study collection itself calls an urban legend: a customer’s dress came back from the dry cleaner mended, with the safety pins in a little bag.

In 2003 she opened the Fashion and Textile Museum in Bermondsey, south London, in a warehouse converted by Ricardo Legorreta, and lives above it. She was made a Dame in 2014.

“I just saw it as an art form. It’s very difficult to cut a piece of fabric to look like a tear. We put beads on the slashes, and they looked lovely. I suppose you could say we put the glam into punk.”

Style codes

  • Matte black, raspberry or red rayon jersey, long and close to the body
  • Deliberate oval tears, raw edges zigzag-stitched in contrast (blue) thread
  • Beaded safety pins, sink-plug ball-chain, scattered diamanté as embroidery
  • Punk as couture: glamour first, aggression filtered out
  • Before and after 1977: hand screen-printed chiffon, squiggles, lipsticks, zig-zags

Signature pieces

  • Conceptual Chic black jersey evening dress, 1977 (V&A T.66-1978) — Sleeveless, 141 cm neck to hem, given by Zandra Rhodes. The V&A notes an identical dress was worn by Debbie Harry in a 1977 London promotional shoot by Sheila Rock.
  • Conceptual Chic long-sleeved black dress, 1977 (V&A T.57-2026) — Courtauld’s rayon jersey, satin-bound neck oxidised to brown; worn by Sandy Stagg. A 2026 acquisition, no image yet.
  • Conceptual Chic evening ensemble, dress and over-vest, 1977–78 (Philadelphia Museum of Art 1998-131-1a,b) — Raspberry rayon knit, safety pins with seed pearls and beads, rhinestones.
