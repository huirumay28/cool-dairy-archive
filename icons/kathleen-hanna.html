Kathleen Hanna

Riot grrrl’s loudest voice, who wrote the insult on her own stomach before anyone could say it. (Added 16 Nov 2026 digest.)

Born 12 November 1968, Portland, Oregon · Bikini Kill 1990–1997 (reunited 2019); Julie Ruin; Le Tigre; The Julie Ruin · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

The person

A first-generation college student at The Evergreen State College in Olympia, Washington, who paid tuition partly by stripping, volunteered at a domestic-violence organisation, and co-founded Reko Muse, a feminist gallery, after the college took down a photo show about sexism and violence against women (Wikipedia, citing Sara Marcus, Girls to the Front).

In October 1990 she formed Bikini Kill with Tobi Vail, Kathi Wilcox and Billy Karren. With Allison Wolfe she organised the first Riot Grrrl meeting; Bikini Kill zine #2 carried the Riot Grrrl Manifesto, and the band’s 1992 zine used the phrase “Girl Power” for the first time (NYU Fales finding aid). At shows she asked girls to come to the front so they would not be crushed or groped in the pit.

The work

Bikini Kill’s records came out on Kill Rock Stars; Joan Jett produced “New Radio” / “Rebel Girl”. After the band she made Julie Ruin alone in her bedroom, then Le Tigre with Johanna Fateman in New York. Her memoir Rebel Girl: My Life as a Feminist Punk came out in May 2024. She designed its soft-looking cover herself, “a kind of middle finger” to the loud image (Surface).

Why the look existed

The body as a page. Photographs had been used to speak for her, so she wrote on herself first. Later she said that move came from privilege, and she said so in public.

“When I started being photographed, I felt like I was silenced by the photograph. … I would get my picture taken and they could write absolutely anything over it, under it, whatever. … I’m going to beat them to the punch by writing this and say, ‘I know you’re thinking that.’”

Terry Gross, Fresh Air (with Johanna Fateman) · 2000

“I was always thinking of what we would look like photographed, which is why I started writing stuff on my stomach, to draw people’s eyes away from the ugly backdrop.”

Surface · 2024

“I wrote “slut” on my stomach a couple of times onstage, and that became sort of a calling card. But later I was — I think rightly — criticized … that I had the privilege as a white woman to stand onstage with the word “slut” written on my body.”

Lyndsey Parker, Yahoo Entertainment · 2021

“It doesn’t mean you’re not a feminist because you expose your legs and wear a leotard.”

Nicole Brodeur, The Seattle Times · 2015

“Stop always worrying about what you look like and what clothes you wear, ’cause in the end it’s not important. What’s important is friendship and being creative.”

quoted in AnOther, “The Riot Grrrl Style Revolution” (original source not given)

What she was reading, hearing, looking at

Feminism as something that “can still save people’s lives” (she worked at a domestic-violence shelter; Yahoo, 2021). Zines: Tobi Vail’s Jigsaw, which she admired before they formed the band. Intersectional feminism in her later interviews: “If it’s not intersectional, it’s not feminism” (Yahoo headline, 2021).

1990–1994

Riot grrrl kit — thrift girlishness, Docs, and words on skin (added 16 Nov 2026 digest)

Before the band, as an Evergreen student in 1989, she made a dress lettered “As he drug her upstairs by her neck” after her housemate was assaulted, the main piece in a fashion show she put on in the college library (AnOther, from The Punk Singer). The writing moved from cloth to skin. The Fresh Air and Surface quotes give two reasons: get ahead of the caption, and pull the eye away from the club’s ugly backdrop. The 2021 quote is her own correction. Hanna told Fresh Air in 2000 that the look was later sold back as “slut” T-shirts by companies probably owned by men, “pseudorebellion”. That makes it a strong caption idea, but not something to sell.

Garments
On stage, often little more than a bra top or short top with SLUT written across her midriff, in lipstick according to The Seattle Times; a girlish ponytail. Around the scene: pigtails and ponytails with coloured plastic baby barrettes (i-D: Kathi Wilcox in short pigtails tied with silky ribbons; Huggy Bear’s Niki Elliott with a pink plastic barrette in her red bob, 1993); hearts dotting the i’s and pink and sequins on handmade zines and T-shirts (AnOther). Words written on arms, hands and stomachs became something girls in the audience did too.

1 May 1991

Sylvester Park, Olympia — print tank, checked mini over bike shorts, striped headband (added 16 Nov 2026 digest)

Bikini Kill playing an outdoor show in their home town, May Day 1991, months before the summer in Washington, DC, when riot grrrl turned into a movement. It is the everyday version of the look, a print tank, a short skirt and bike shorts, and the easiest one to rebuild from a vintage rail.

Garments
Visible in the two Commons photos: a sleeveless scoop-neck tank in a white-and-blue leaf print; a short white checked skirt over black bike shorts; a striped headband with her hair piled up; a small tattoo on her upper arm; a red bass guitar. Bandmate on the left in a printed short-sleeve shirt and denim cut-offs.
