Poly Styrene

Day-glo plastic against consumerism: proof you could be stylish without money.

X-Ray Spex 1976–78 (era file) · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

The person

Poly Styrene fronted X-Ray Spex. Before the band she ran a stall in Beaufort Market off the King’s Road, near SEX, “selling plastic trash, mainly kitsch jewellery and bags, old army cast-offs and day-glo accessories – all the stuff I later wore on stage.”

Her daughter Celeste Bell says Poly wanted to prove you could be stylish without money — by making your own clothes and buying second-hand.

The work

Her lyrics, in her words, were “painting a picture of consumerism with words.” While punk wore black leather, she wore granny cardigans, shift dresses, ankle socks, an army helmet and braces on her teeth.

Why the look existed

Anti-glamour. Plastic, army surplus and thrift as a joke on what shopping promises.
