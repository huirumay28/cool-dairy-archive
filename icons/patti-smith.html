Patti Smith

She wanted to look like a poet, not a girl singer. She did not have money. She had a picture of Baudelaire and a clean shirt.

Born 1946 · 1974– · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

The person

Patti Smith came out of New York as a poet who also had a band. Horses (1975) is the record and the photograph: Robert Mapplethorpe, a Salvation Army men’s white shirt, cuffs cut off the one that recalled Brassaï’s Genet, a black jacket flung Sinatra-style. Arista wanted a bouffant and the lip hair airbrushed. She refused.

She found Rimbaud in a Philadelphia bus depot at sixteen because the cover looked like it glittered. She dressed like Baudelaire because she liked dressing like Baudelaire, not because she was thinking she would break boundaries. Later the black-and-white is Ann Demeulemeester, worn until it is sweat and holes.

The work

Horses, Radio Ethiopia, Easter and “Because the Night” (written with Bruce Springsteen). Then a long quiet in Detroit, then the return. Since she began performing again after Ann mailed three white shirts from Antwerp, she has said she has never gone onstage without wearing a piece by Ann. They are talismanic.

The Horses shirt and Ann’s three white shirts are one friendship. Do not flatten her into a muse caption. She is the poet the house found in a record-shop window.

Why the look existed

She wanted to look like a poet, not a girl singer. She did not have money. She had a picture of Baudelaire and a clean shirt.

“I had no sense of how it would look, just that it should be true. The only thing I think I promised Robert is that I would wear a clean shirt with no stains on it.”

Just Kids, quoted in Vogue · 2014

“I thought of myself as a poet and a performer, and so how did I dress? I didn’t have much money; I liked to dress like Baudelaire. I looked at a picture of him and he was dressed, like, with this ribbon or tie and a white shirt. I wasn’t thinking that I was going to break any boundaries. I just like dressing like Baudelaire.”

The Guardian · 1996

“I derive great power from wearing Ann’s clothes. Since I began performing again, after that first gift, I have never gone onstage without wearing a piece by Ann… They make me feel confident, they make me feel truly like myself. They are talismanic.”

Vogue, with Ann Demeulemeester · 2014

“I found him in a Philadelphia bus depot when I was sixteen. I remember seeing a copy of Illuminations for sale on a table of used books. Of course, illuminations is a great word, but what I was really taken by was the cover. It was a beautiful picture of Rimbaud. That’s why I got the book.”

Ingrid Sischy, Interview

“My Keith Richards haircut was a real discourse magnet.”

Just Kids (memoir, 2010), on Max’s Kansas City

“I was in my Keith Richards stage and I was really flashy, but you know I'm not really a great singer; I'm not really tough; I'm not really a leather man; I'm a girl”

Oui interview (oceanstar transcript) · 1976

What she was reading, hearing, looking at

Rimbaud first (crush, then language). Genet via the Brassaï picture on the shirt. Baudelaire’s cravat. In Patti Smith Complete she also lists June Christy’s ponytail, a raincoat à la Camus, Dylan’s snap-tab collar, Ava Gardner’s black capris. To Rolling Stone, 1 January 1976, the same Rimbaud cover “looked so cool, just like Bob Dylan.”

c. 1969–70 (Just Kids era)

The Keith Richards haircut at Max’s Kansas City (added 26 Oct 2026 digest)

In Just Kids she writes that the cut “caused quite a stir” at Max’s and that her social status went up overnight though she was “still the same person.” The book describes it as cutting her way out of the folk era. A haircut, not a garment, turned a bookshop clerk into the androgynous figure the Horses cover later fixed in place.

Garments
Hair chopped herself into a shag copied from magazine photos of Keith Richards. The look around it: men’s jackets, white shirts, skinny ties, a thin frame. The 1976 Zürich photo shows where it led: a black men’s blazer over a dark tee, wristwatch, the same shag.
Designer / season
none. Self-cut hair, menswear basics.
Where it lives
