Siouxsie Sioux

Bromley Contingent punk who dressed as armour and refused to become anyone’s uniform. (Added 9 Nov 2026 digest.)

Born 1957 (Susan Ballion); grew up in Chislehurst, near Bromley, south-east London · Siouxsie and the Banshees 1976–1996; The Creatures; solo · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

The person

A suburban teenager who loved David Bowie, Marc Bolan and Bette Davis, and hired outfits from a local costume shop at weekends because she had little money for clothes (The Independent, 2003). Fred Perry’s subculture series adds Catwoman’s black catsuit, Emma Peel’s leather in The Avengers, and Cabaret.

In 1975–76 she was one of the Bromley Contingent, the early Sex Pistols crowd. “It wasn’t a fan club,” she told Magnet in 2007. “It was like-minded people meeting at a lot of gay clubs, actually, who had gotten into the Pistols.” She stood behind the Pistols on Bill Grundy’s Today show in December 1976, the broadcast that made punk a tabloid story.

The work

Siouxsie and the Banshees began as a one-off, unrehearsed 20-minute set at the 100 Club Punk Festival in September 1976, with Steve Severin, Marco Pirroni and Sid Vicious, including a dirge version of the Lord’s Prayer. She had never sung in public before. The band lasted twenty years; Kaleidoscope (1980) and Juju (1981) are the records that turned punk into what came to be called goth, a label the band itself resisted.

Why the look existed

Dressing up as armour and as a test of the people looking at her. She rejected the idea of a punk or goth uniform even while thousands of girls copied her eye make-up.

“To me dressing up was a natural form of expression but it was also a useful armour. I think I also saw my image as a way of covering up my lack of actual beauty. I didn’t want people to see the real me.”

The Independent, “Return of the Creature” · 2003

“I remember feeling like that when punk went tabloid and people started wearing bloody safety pins. They turned it into a cartoon. We had never dressed like that, for us it was never about being part of some army.”

The Independent · 2003

“Back then I liked the fact that people were looking at me and wondering ‘Is she serious or is she being funny?’”

The Independent · 2003

What she was reading, hearing, looking at

Bowie, Marc Bolan, Roxy Music, Bette Davis, Catwoman, Emma Peel, Cabaret (The Independent; Fred Perry). Later friendship and costume work with Pam Hogg, who made the Japanese-inspired, washable Dreamshow costume from Siouxsie’s own drawings.

20 September 1976

100 Club Punk Festival — slashed T-shirt, her sister’s pinstripe jacket (added 9 Nov 2026 digest)

The first Banshees gig was an act of nerve, not a debut: “Fifteen or 20 minutes, that was all the future that I saw” (Magnet, 2007). The look is thrift tailoring plus damage, which is exactly a vintage rail. Caution: some 1976 photos show a swastika armband (mentioned in press reprinted on the Banshees fan archive) — do not use those images.

Garments
Slashed T-shirt, her sister’s pinstripe jacket, and a black star painted over one eye (The Independent). The wider 1976 kit, per Fred Perry: oversized men’s suit jackets, black fishnet stockings, stilettos, a see-through mesh blouse worn without a bra, T-shirts and boots from Westwood and McLaren’s SEX, spiky peroxide or black hair, heavy black eyeliner.

1980

Kaleidoscope year on tour — fringed jacket, fishnets, the backcombed black hair (added 9 Nov 2026 digest)

The hinge between 1976 punk and the early-80s look Fred Perry credits with defining goth for young women: crimped, backcombed black hair, PVC minis, fishnets, belts and bangles, white face and heavy black eyes. Western fringe on a punk body is the cool dairy angle.

Garments
My Father’s Place, New York, Nov 1980 (Commons caption): glossy black western-style jacket with a white yoke and long fringe, fishnet tights, fringed black hip piece, backcombed black hair, white face, black-winged eyes, red lip. Edinburgh Tiffany’s, 1980: black leather jacket, the same hair and eyes.
Designer / season
none / unsourced. No maker credited for either jacket.

1981–1980s

Cult objects in the V&A — bootleg photo tee, Juju tour poster, Mortisha’s BEASTS jewellery (added 9 Nov 2026 digest)

This is the Cult tier on paper: a bootleg face tee, a gig poster and a woman-run goth label from Carnaby Street, all accessioned. The PWP tee is the template for every Siouxsie face tee on a vintage rail.

Garments
PWP London T-shirt (V&A T.620-1997): white cotton tee printed with a monochrome photo of Siouxsie, hand-touched with blue eyeshadow and red lips. Juju tour poster (V&A S.4397-1995): teal and black, Hemel Hempstead Pavilion, Wednesday 2 September 1981. BEASTS, “a legend in clothing for the living dead”: the label Carole “Mortisha” Ascoli launched in 1981, selling from the Flea Market on Carnaby Street, then The Great Frog, then Camden Lock. The V&A records that she designed and made costumes for Siouxsie and the Banshees. Spider necklace T.637-1995 and fly earrings T.54-2014 are 1995 remakes of 1981 designs; coffin bag T.636-1995.
Designer / season
Tee: PWP London, 1980s (V&A) — no designer. Jewellery: Carole Mortisha Ascoli (V&A designer credit), designed 1981, remade 1995. Which Banshees costumes Mortisha made is not itemised — do not invent one.
