Icon

Gwen Stefani

A girl in a boys’ ska band who dressed as both at once — screen-siren face, skater-boy from the waist down.

Born 1969 · 1986– · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

The person

Gwen Stefani wrote “Just a Girl” from inside No Doubt, an Anaheim ska-punk band that had been slogging clubs for years. The 1995 look was not a stylist’s idea of a tomboy. It was what she already wore, plus one girly thing, because the song was about the divide.

Homemade first: Dickies, chain belt, initial belt, thrift tennis top, press-on letters. Then archival Westwood once she could buy it — often without knowing the house at the till. Bindis, platinum, red lip, tartan, corset as daywear.

The work

Tragic Kingdom (1995) made the band inescapable. Just a Girl, Spiderwebs, Don’t Speak. She has said there was nobody telling them what to wear. Later, Love. Angel. Music. Baby. name-checks Vivienne Westwood, Comme des Garçons, and Galliano’s Dior.

The bindi story is hers: Tony Kanal’s mother. Personal, not a house code. Later readers will argue cultural appropriation; she has always told this as a family story. Keep both facts.

Why the look existed

A girl in a boys’ ska band who felt the power and the danger of being female, and dressed as both at once — screen-siren face, skater-boy from the waist down.

“I was so naïve when I wrote ‘Just A Girl.’ But it was about that moment when you realize the power in being a female and also the vulnerability in being a female and the things you can’t do because it might not be safe or people might not take you seriously.”

Liam Hess, Vogue, 25th anniversary of the video · 2020

“There was nobody telling us what to wear. That was just me literally wearing exactly the clothes that I would wear every day.”

Vogue · 2020

The pants were just a pair of Dickies I always used to wear, and I ended up gluing or sewing that stripe down the leg of the pants. The G belt is literally just one of those belts you can get that has your initial on it. And the top I’m wearing inside the car that says ‘Anaheim,’ I made that.”

What she was reading, hearing, looking at

Bindis: Tony Kanal’s mother. “His family is Indian, and I grew up with him from age 17 to 26… she used to wear bindis. She gave me a bunch, and I just thought they were so beautiful, and I started making it my thing.” Personal, not a house. Keep the later argument in the same sentence.

1995–97

Tragic Kingdom — making her own stage clothes (added 19 Oct 2026 digest)

Staying up all night to sew for tomorrow’s show: the look was handmade before it was famous.

Garments
3 or 4 dresses from her own pattern — “a corset-style drop waist with a cheerleader skirt” — in bright primary colours, worn over boxers with fishnets and Docs. Then the switch away from dresses (she moves too much on stage) to men’s work pants. Her mum sewed most of her clothes growing up; together they copied Grace Kelly’s Rear Window dress for her prom.
Designer / season
Self-made. By 1996 WWD reported her working with LA designer Deborah Viereck, whose label ’T ain’t also made custom pieces for Marilyn Manson. No season.
