Icon

Karen O

Not fashion. A Superman change: the garment lets Karen Orzolek leave the room and Karen O walk on.

Born 1978 (Karen Lee Orzolek) · 2000– · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

The person

Karen O fronts Yeah Yeah Yeahs. The dress is how a shy, modest person becomes the person who can explode. Christian Joy builds the costume; Karen has to live up to it.

The collaboration starts in 2001: Karen sees Joy’s deconstructed prom dresses and asks for one for a show at The Cooler in Meatpacking. Joy: one night, a tattered blue prom dress. “Garbagey” / car-crash prom — spray paint, rip, dollar-store materials, spandex for movement. Theatrical makeup: neon lid, red mouth, choppy bob.

The work

Fever to Tell (2003) and “Maps” put the band on MTV2 and then everywhere. Show Your Bones, It’s Blitz!, Cool It Down. The wardrobe stays Joy’s: no commercial runway on the priority looks.

To Teen Vogue she said being half Korean made her feel like an outsider, and that shaped her as a rocker. Music is how a shy kid talks. Christian Joy, not Karen, is the one who said the costume is how she “suddenly becomes Karen O.” Use Joy’s line as a designer note, not as Karen’s words.

Why the look existed

Not fashion. A Superman change: the garment lets Karen Orzolek leave the room and Karen O walk on.

“She made me a deconstructed car crash prom dress. I remember thinking, ‘Please, rock gods, let me live up to this dress.’ I didn’t know if I could pull it off, but I did. The rest is history.”

Andrew Bevan, Teen Vogue, Mosquito era · 2013

“I’m actually extremely modest and conservative. I never like to show any skin. My worst nightmare is my boob popping out. So when it came to the Show Your Bones costume, which was 90% spandex, that was definitely outside my comfort zone.”

Teen Vogue · 2013

“It was about feeling kind of tattered or torn. I wanted it to feel really exuberant but also a little bit shredded up from everything we’ve been through.”

Vogue, on the Cool It Down tour wardrobe · 2022

What she was reading, hearing, looking at

To Teen Vogue: “For me, being half Korean made me feel like an outsider, and that has really shaped my career as a rocker. I think a lot of us in rock are misfits.” She also names Elton John and Grace Jones as the stylish people she admires.
