Olivia Rodrigo

Archival 80s/90s as a Gen-Z default, after a documentary killed the mall habit.

Born 2003 · 2020– · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

The person

Olivia Rodrigo made Sour (2021) and Guts (2023) as a pop writer who treats stage clothes as a heightened version of how she already feels. The inner reason she gives is not “I collect Westwood.” It is: I used to buy crap to fill a void; now I wear other people’s clothes and tell the album story at a higher volume.

Sour-era stylists: Chloe & Chenelle Delgadillo. Guts: Jared Ellner. Corset + tartan mini + combat boot + choker is her repeating sentence. She has said she loves Gwen Stefani / Return of Saturn, Lorde, Taylor Swift, and riot-grrrl babydolls — Courtney Love, Kat Bjelland.

Why the look existed

Stage and video clothes are a heightened version of how she already feels — riot-grrrl babydoll, archival corset, Sevigny’s tartan — after a documentary killed the mall habit.

“I watched this documentary called The True Cost [2015] and at that point, I was getting into vegetarianism, veganism and everyday things you could do to be more sustainable in your life, but I’d never thought about how our clothes can have such a detrimental effect on the environment. Back then, I was a huge shopaholic. I’d go to the mall and buy a bunch of crap I didn’t need to fill a void — it felt good to have things — but then I watched the film and it completely changed my perspective. So now most of my clothes are secondhand or from sustainable sellers.”

British Vogue · 2021

“I really love the idea of a babydoll [dress]. I just remember being younger and having pictures of Courtney Love and Kat Bjelland from all these riot grrrl punk bands in their babydoll dresses, just owning it. I always thought that that was really cool.”

Vogue, on the Drop Dead / Barça costuming · 2026

“I think everything that I wear on stage and in videos is a really heightened version of how I feel in my day-to-day life. I mean, every day when I wake up and I’m just going to the studio, I keep it pretty chill and keep it pretty jeans-and-a-T-shirt. But it’s really nice to express myself through clothes and tell stories through clothes because I think that that’s a huge part of an album rollout.”

Vogue · 2026

“I’ve had this dress, the picture of her wearing this dress, saved on my Pinterest for years, and I was actually able to get my hands on it. I’m so lucky—it’s my most prized possession.”

Vogue 73 Questions, on Chloë Sevigny’s tartan Versace · 2023
