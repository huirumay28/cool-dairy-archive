Blondie

Hollywood blonde as a neon sign, Bowery dirt left in the roots.

Born 1945 (Debbie Harry) · 1974– · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

The person

Debbie Harry fronted Blondie out of downtown New York: CBGB, the Bowery, a platinum sign she later called walking around with her own spotlight. The blonde was silver-screen on purpose. The roots were left in.

She wanted each show to happen, then she wanted one identity that would stay in people’s minds. Sexy or funny, never tidy. Stephen Sprouse, a neighbour, organised the trademark after she had been wearing millions of things.

The work

Blondie took new wave onto the pop charts. Parallel Lines (1978) and “Heart of Glass” made Harry a face the cameras already knew how to read. The band made videos before MTV because they were big in Australia and Europe and could not always travel.

The look is not a side project. Harry has said that wearing millions of things made each show happen but limited the identity; Sprouse made it concise. Underwear as outerwear, Day-Glo, a TV-static print — Bowery dirt under Hollywood chrome.

Why the look existed

She wanted each show to happen, then she wanted one identity that would stay in people’s minds. Sexy or funny, never tidy. Felt right, looked hot.

“The blonde thing is really very much about the old silver screen platinum blonde, and I certainly was interested in capturing that as the frontwoman for a band.”

Interview, Life Lessons

“What I used to do – which was a mistake, I think – was wear millions and millions of things that were never really a trademark, but would make each show happen; it was very artistic but very limited. Now it’s very concise, and everything is sort of organised so that the identity of what I am as the lead singer of Blondie stays in people’s minds.”

Berry Berenson Perkins, Manhattan Cable TV; quoted by BBC Culture · 1979

“I think he just couldn’t bear seeing me look like a mess, so he would help me. Friends would say to me, ‘no not that. Or why don’t you add a bangle?’ I don’t think I ever hired a professional stylist.”

The Telegraph, on Stephen Sprouse

“I almost got thrown off a bus once for wearing my underwear. The bus driver screamed at me. I had on little orange satiny-pink tap pants – they looked fantastic, but he was outraged that I was walking around in my skivvies. I remember using my bra as outerwear, and really getting a lot of bad looks. It just felt right. It looked hot.”

The New York Times, around the Met’s 2013 PUNK show; quoted by BBC Culture · 2013

What she was reading, hearing, looking at

Early stage clothes were second-hand and campy — everything as sexy as possible or as outrageous and funny as possible. Sprouse’s first dress for her: a black knit one-shoulder thing, a dance tight he altered. Meeting him she later called a magnificent coincidence for her whole career.
