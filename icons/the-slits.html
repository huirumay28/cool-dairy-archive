The Slits

Four young women who wore SEX rubber, school gym skirts, ballet tutus and work boots together so that men on the street could not read them.

1976–82 (era file, added 2 Nov 2026 digest) · filed Sunday 16 August 2026

The person

Added 2 Nov 2026 digest. The Slits were Ari Up (Ariane Forster, vocals), Viv Albertine (guitar), Tessa Pollitt (bass) and Palmolive (Paloma Romero, drums; later Budgie). They toured on the Clash’s White Riot tour in 1977 and released Cut on Island in September 1979, produced by Dennis Bovell at Ridge Farm. Rolling Stone credits the record with making Bikini Kill and Sleater-Kinney possible.

Albertine on how they wrote: they could not jam, so they asked of every note, “Does it excite you? Does it unsettle you?” — “unpicking all your habits.” She says the Vivienne Westwood lesson came first: watching Westwood make clothes “with such abandon” without being taught convinced her she could write songs that weren’t perfect.

The work

Cut (1979) fuses punk with reggae and dub; their favourite record while making it was Dionne Warwick Sings Burt Bacharach, which they took apart. The cover was shot by Pennie Smith in the Ridge Farm rose garden: the three of them topless, smeared in mud, in loincloths. Albertine: “We wanted a warrior stance, to be a tribe… We knew, since we had no clothes on, that we had to look confrontational and hard. We didn’t want to be inviting the male gaze.”

The look was dangerous to wear. Hotels on the White Riot tour made them walk straight from the door to the lift. Ari Up, then 15, was stabbed on the street twice by strangers within a year (Albertine to The Guardian, 2014).

Why the look existed

Mixing pieces that each signalled ‘girl’ but were never worn together, so the result read as neither available nor pretty. Albertine: “It was very confusing for men.”

“I’d mix that with the tutu I wore to ballet lessons when I was a little child and men’s work boots. The whole look was so threatening to straight men that they couldn’t bear it.”

Viv Albertine to i-D

“You could take stuff like fetish wear and you could wear it in a subversive way. It was a political statement… They knew we were saying ‘fuck you’ and taking our bodies back.”

Viv Albertine to Wylde Magazine · 2017

“We said things with our clothes, we shook up expectations, we laughed at conventions… Nothing was expected to be perfect.”

Viv Albertine to i-D

“All these girls are so groomed and polished and are being put out there as an industry or as a gimmick. It is scary to think that this is how women are meant to look.”

Ari Up to Loud And Quiet

What she was reading, hearing, looking at

Albertine called Vivienne Westwood “the most inspiring person I’ve ever met” and copied her hair until it became her own. Later she was drawn to the Rastas in her neighbourhood and reggae — but refused the Rasta women’s long brown skirts and Earth shoes, and had a roots hat made from the boys’ fashions instead. Her 2014 memoir is titled Clothes, Clothes, Clothes. Music, Music, Music. Boys, Boys, Boys.

1977–79

SEX, sex shops, school uniform and a ballet tutu (added 2 Nov 2026 digest)

The first all-woman punk band to look like this, and the template riot grrrl later inherited: girlish signifiers made hostile.

Garments
Viv Albertine, in her own captions: Johnny Thunders’ skull T-shirt (his favourite; she still has it), a studded belt from a hardcore S&M shop, her grey school gym miniskirt, thigh-high boots made at the bootmaker Westwood used. On stage 1978: pink 1950s tutu, white T-shirt with the arms pinned up with badges, sheer pink tights, socks and trainers, coloured ribbons in her hair, Rickenbacker. On tour: Tessa in opaque black leggings and pink pumps, Ari in red pumps — cheap shoes from New York’s Chinatown — Ari in “everything she owns that is spotty”, Tessa in a cocktail dress with the hem hacked off. Band kit generally: fetish wear, slashed T-shirts, Doc Martens, hair standing on end.
Designer / season
Mostly none — self-assembled, customised, sex-shop and school pieces. SEX (Westwood & McLaren, 430 King’s Road) was the only shop they bought from, one piece every few months; no specific SEX garment is credited here.
Where it lives
Albertine says she still owns the Johnny Thunders skull T-shirt (BuzzFeed). No Slits garment found in a museum collection.
